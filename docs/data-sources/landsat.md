## What it is

Landsat 8 and 9 (USGS + NASA) photograph every field on Earth at 30 m every 8 days between them, in the red and near-infrared bands that make NDVI (plant greenness), back to 1982 with older satellites.
HLS (Harmonized Landsat Sentinel-2, NASA LP DAAC) merges Landsat 8/9 with Europe's Sentinel-2 into one 30 m record with a pass every 2 to 3 days, and now also publishes ready-made vegetation indices (HLS-VI: NDVI, EVI and seven more).

## Where the second check differed

- Esri Landsat image service identify (NDVI Raw at Barrie): the first check got 0.0600042, the second got 0.521332 with the same request. The value depends on which scene the mosaic picks at the moment of the call; it is not a stable number.
- HLS Fmask 68 at Barrie (Planetary Computer point read): the value is right, but 68 = bit 2 (next to cloud/shadow) + bit 6 (aerosol low), so the pixel is not flagged fully clear. Worth knowing before showing NDVI 0.53 as a clean reading.
- CMR STAC for HLS returns the oldest item in the date range first.
- Everything else matched the first check.

## Problems

1. HLS is 9 days behind: the newest HLS file anywhere in the world is dated 2026-11-05 (published 2026-11-11), for both L30 and S30; over Barrie the newest are 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01. The GIBS HLS layers end 2026-11-06. Usual delay is 2 to 4 days (I think).
2. USGS's own files need a USGS (ERS) login, which is a different account from the NASA Earthdata login; the AWS copy is requester-pays.
3. Earth Search rejects a sort on `datetime` for landsat-c2-l2 (400); without the sort it works.
4. The Planetary Computer HLS copy starts in 2020 (NASA's starts 2013 for L30 and 2015 for S30), and its terms and limits could not be read.
5. AppEEARS's plain Landsat products are the US-only ARD (I think): for Ontario, AppEEARS means HLS.
6. Old GIBS Landsat layers (WELD) end in 2001.
7. The Esri NDVI value at a point changed between the two checks (0.0600042, then 0.521332): it is not stable.

## Recommendation

Keep HLS as the field-greenness source, but switch the read to the HLS-VI NDVI file (one read instead of red + NIR) with the NASA Earthdata login, and use the Planetary Computer point API (no login, about 1 s per scene, Landsat C2 L2 and HLS) as the route that works today and as the fallback when the login or LP DAAC fails.
Show the farmer the same "greenness of your field" dots, plus a real 30 m picture of the field from the GIBS HLS layer for the chosen date.

## How Crophalo uses it

- Crophalo plans to search CMR for HLSL30 and HLSS30 v2.0 (last 365 days, cloud below 60 %), then read small windows of the Fmask, red and near-infrared bands from LP DAAC with the Earthdata login and compute NDVI (at most 36 scenes). It also plans to use OPERA DSWx-HLS for water on the field. Landsat itself (Collection 2) is not used.
- Not used yet: HLS-VI ready-made indices (NDVI, EVI, NDMI = crop water, NBR = burn / residue, SAVI, MSAVI); the Planetary Computer no-login point read; Landsat surface temperature bands (`lwir11` in landsat-c2-l2, checked in the asset list) at 30 m (resampled from 100 m, I think); the GIBS 30 m HLS picture layers and the OPERA DIST-ALERT layer; the Landsat archive back to 1982 for field history; AppEEARS HLS point series.
