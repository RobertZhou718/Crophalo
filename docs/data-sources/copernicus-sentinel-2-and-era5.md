## What they are

Sentinel-2 is Europe's pair (now trio) of land-imaging satellites: a 10 m picture of every field on Earth every 2 to 5 days, in 13 colours, free. It is what shows one field's greenness, bare soil, flooding or hail damage.
ERA5 is ECMWF's rebuilt weather record for the whole world, hourly since 1940 on a 25 to 31 km grid (ERA5-Land: 9 km since 1950), about 5 days behind. Both cover Ontario.

## Terms

**Sentinel-2 data** (read, the Commission's "Legal notice on the use of Copernicus Sentinel Data and Service Information"): free, full and open access; reproduction, distribution, communication to the public, adaptation and combination are all allowed, with no limit to non-commercial use. A free public site may use it: yes. A business may: yes. Credit: "Copernicus Sentinel data [Year]", or when changed (an NDVI is a change) **"Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data [Year]"**. No warranty.

**The services that hand it out differ:**
- Earth Search (Element 84 on AWS): collection licence field points to the Sentinel legal notice (checked). No terms page or rate limit read; it is a free community service with no promise of uptime (I think).
- Planetary Computer: terms and SAS pages are script-rendered and could **not be read**. Anonymous use is rate-limited and signatures last about an hour (I think; the second check saw an expiry about 45 minutes ahead). Its Sentinel-2 licence link still points at the dead `scihub.copernicus.eu` (checked).
- CDSE free account, "Copernicus General" quotas (read, `documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/Quotas.html`): Sentinel Hub 10,000 requests and 10,000 processing units a month, 300 a minute; openEO 10,000 credits a month ("temporary boost"), 2 concurrent jobs, 12 requests a minute; downloads 4 connections at 20 MB/s, 12 TB a month; 50,000 direct COG HTTP requests a month; tokens live 10 minutes; making several accounts to dodge quotas is forbidden (read on the terms page). The terms page says Sentinel data are "free, full and open" and other portal content is "intended for non-commercial use"; whether a business may serve its customers from one free account is **not stated** (read).
- EOX cloudless tiles (read, `cloudless.eox.at/documentation/license`): **non-commercial only** under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; commercial use needs a paid EOX licence; credit "EOxCloudless https://cloudless.eox.at by EOX IT Services GmbH (Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data {year})". Fine for the free site with credit; not for the business.
- NASA HLS, GIBS, AppEEARS: NASA open data, free for any use (I think; not re-read).

**ERA5 data**: CC-BY-4.0, as stated in the CDS catalogue record for every ERA5 dataset listed (checked). Free public site: yes. Business: yes. Credit (I think, not read): "Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information [Year]" plus the dataset DOI (Hersbach et al.). CDS needs each dataset's terms accepted once on its page before the API will serve it (read).

**Open-Meteo** (read, `open-meteo.com/en/terms`): the free API is for **non-commercial use**: "Less than 10'000 API calls per day, 5'000 per hour and 600 per minute" (300,000 a month in its table); non-commercial means "Private or non-profit websites or apps that do not have subscriptions or advertising"; commercial is "Websites or apps that have subscriptions or display advertisements" and needs a paid plan. Data licence CC BY 4.0 with credit to Open-Meteo. So: the free ad-free site is inside the free terms; the later business is not.

## Problems

- CDSE OpenSearch (`/resto/…`) answers 404, also for its own documentation example: older tutorials that use it are dead. The old CDSE STAC address answers 404 for the old collection name SENTINEL-2, but works as an alias with the new name `sentinel-2-l2a`. Use `stac.dataspace.copernicus.eu/v1`.
- CDSE STAC took 1.4 s then 4.1 s (2.88 s in the second check) for one scene and returns 72 kB per scene; its band links are `s3://` addresses that need account keys.
- Planetary Computer's blobs refuse without a signature (409) and its documentation pages could not be read by a plain fetch.
- NASA HLS ran 10 days behind the raw Sentinel-2 catalogues over Barrie (newest HLS 1 Nov, newest L2A 11 Nov). The newest raw scene was 95% cloud, so the farmer would not have gained a clear look this time, but the lag is real.
- Esri's Sentinel-2 identify returned "NoData" in the first check and timed out in the second; its exportImage gave a blank picture. Not usable as tried.
- EOX cloudless is technically open but legally non-commercial.
- ERA5 is 5 to 6 days behind (Open-Meteo values end 7 to 8 Nov; CDS says data to 8 Nov).
- Through Open-Meteo, `era5_land` has no rain and no ET0 (nulls for any date): rain must come from `era5`.
- The old AWS `era5-pds` bucket is closed (403). NCAR's THREDDS catalogue took 13 s (24.2 s in the second check).
- CDS gives nothing without an account and queues requests: unsuitable as a live per-visitor call.
- Open-Meteo free terms end when the site earns money (Crophalo already plans for this; see below).

## Recommendation

Sentinel-2: keep NASA HLS as the NASA-first source, and add a keyless second look from Earth Search (search) with the Planetary Computer point call or a byte-range COG read for red, near-infrared and SCL, to show "Greenness of your field, 10 m, newest clear photo 3 days ago" with the credit "Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2026"; use GIBS HLS tiles for a dated true-colour picture, and avoid EOX tiles in the business.
ERA5: stay with the Open-Meteo archive (`models=era5` for the back-up record, `era5_land` for the cold night only), budget the paid plan or a self-hosted Open-Meteo from its AWS bucket before charging money, and consider AgERA5 from the CDS (with Crophalo's own free account, fetched by the server, not per visitor) only if a 10 km farm-weather record is wanted.

## How Crophalo uses it

- Sentinel-2: Crophalo plans to use it only inside NASA HLS v2.0 (HLSS30 bands B04 and B8A, found by CMR point search, last 365 days, cloud 0 to 60%, 30 m NDVI in a 3x3 window, with the NASA Earthdata login), and OPERA DSWx-HLS for water. Raw 10 m Sentinel-2 from Copernicus, Earth Search or Planetary Computer, openEO, Sentinel Hub and the GIBS HLS picture layer are not used. Planetary Computer is used only for NASADEM heights. Earth Search and the Planetary Computer point read are keyless extras.
- ERA5: Crophalo already uses the Open-Meteo archive `models=era5` as the back-up weather record when NASA POWER fails, and `models=era5_land` daily minimum temperature as the second coldest-night record; both fit what answered on 2026-11-14 (era5_land has no rain or ET0). Crophalo already knows the free Open-Meteo terms are non-commercial and has priced a paid plan at about $30 a month. The CDS API, AgERA5, the point time-series datasets and the Google, AWS and NCAR mirrors are not used.
