## What it is

In February 2000 the Space Shuttle's radar (SRTM) measured the height of the land between 60 N and 56 S on a grid about 30 m wide; NASADEM (2020) is NASA's cleaned-up reprocessing of the same flight, and ASTER GDEM is a separate 30 m map from stereo photos that reaches 83 N.
All are "surface" maps: they include the tops of trees and buildings, not the bare ground. NASA's own copies sit behind the Earthdata login; several open mirrors serve the same numbers with no login.

## Terms

- NASA SRTM / NASADEM / ASTER GDEM (LP DAAC): NASA's data-use page says data from NASA-led missions are **CC0** unless marked otherwise, "no restrictions on the use", citation "very strongly urged"; NASA's name may not be used to imply endorsement (read, `earthdata.nasa.gov/engage/open-data-services-software-policies/data-use-guidance`). Free site: yes. Business: yes. Credit: cite the product DOI, e.g. NASADEM `10.5067/MEaSUREs/NASADEM/NASADEM_HGT.001`, SRTM `10.5067/MEaSUREs/SRTM/SRTMGL1.003` (DOIs checked in CMR). The LP DAAC "citing our data" page that was tried returned 404 (not read).
- OpenTopography API (read, `opentopography.org/developers`): a key is required; the free key is limited to **200 calls per 24 hours for academics and 50 for non-academics**; 30 m datasets up to 450,000 km2 per request; keys may not be shared or embedded in an application that lets third parties use it; "integration of the API into commercial software packages" needs a paid **Enterprise API key**. So: not usable for a public site or a business without a paid agreement. The public bucket's own terms: not read.
- Planetary Computer: collection metadata says licence "proprietary" with NASA/JPL/USGS as licensors and OpenTopography as processor (checked). The terms page is a JavaScript app and could not be read by curl (not read). The service is a free preview with no service promise (I think).
- AWS Terrain Tiles (read, AWS registry page and Tilezen `attribution.md`): attribution is **required**; the line for our areas includes "United States 3DEP (formerly NED) and global GMTED2010 and SRTM terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey" and for Canada "contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence - Canada" (first words read; full Canadian line not copied). Cite: "Terrain Tiles was accessed on DATE from https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles". Free site: yes. Business: yes with attribution. No stated rate limit (plain S3).
- Open-Meteo (read): free API is for **non-commercial use only**, under 10,000 calls a day, 5,000 an hour, 600 a minute, CC BY 4.0. A business needs their paid plan.
- Open Topo Data public API: limits not read (I think 1 call a second and 1,000 a day).
- GIBS: no key; NASA asks for the credit "We acknowledge the use of imagery provided by services from NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS), part of NASA's Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS)" (I think; not re-read).

## Problems

- NASA's own SRTM and NASADEM files are behind the Earthdata login (302 to urs.earthdata.nasa.gov) and are zipped .hgt tiles, not cloud-optimised: each read means a whole 5.7 MB download. The old open server (`e4ftl01.cr.usgs.gov`) is gone; everything is now LPCLOUD (I think it was retired with the cloud move; not re-checked).
- OpenTopography's API refuses without a key (401) and its free key is 50 calls a day for a non-academic and may not be built into a public app: **not a route for Crophalo**. Its public bucket does answer without a key.
- The AWS terrain tile at Barrie is built from Canada's CDEM (2017 build), not SRTM. Calling the browser layer "SRTM" in Ontario is not accurate; Crophalo already describes it as "CDEM about 20 m in Canada" and should keep that wording.
- Planetary Computer blobs refuse reads without the signature (409), and the collection's licence field says "proprietary" although the data are NASA's.
- USGS point service: one timeout (504 after 29 s) and one 16 s answer in the first check; 18.5 s in the second.
- Open Topo Data has no NASADEM dataset (400).
- LP DAAC "citing our data" page: 404. The Earthdata site also showed a banner on 2026-11-14: "Earthdata Forum is down due to unplanned maintenance".
- SRTM/NASADEM stop at 60 N (northern Canada, Scandinavia are not covered) and are from February 2000; they are surface maps (trees and buildings read as hills).

## Recommendation

Keep what Crophalo has: AWS Terrain Tiles in the browser and the finest-first chain on the server (lidar, then GLO-30, NASADEM last through Planetary Computer). For a NASA-named height with no login, add the Planetary Computer point call (one keyless JSON answer, 0.6-1.4 s) or window reads of the OpenTopography bucket's NASADEM/SRTM GeoTIFFs as a second mirror.
Show the farmer the field's height, slope and where water runs, and name the true source per place (CDEM in Canada, SRTM/NASADEM elsewhere); do not use the OpenTopography API or Open-Meteo's free elevation call once the site is a business.

## How Crophalo uses it

- Crophalo already uses AWS terrarium tiles, decoded in the browser, for its slope, erosion, frost-pocket, relief and shade layers.
- On the server, Crophalo's height lookup has 10 sources tried finest first; NASADEM (read from Planetary Computer with a keyless signature) is the last fallback after lidar and Copernicus GLO-30. GLO-30 is also used for the far horizon.
- Not used yet: the Planetary Computer point API, the OpenTopography bucket, the skadi/geotiff tile formats, AppEEARS, GIBS relief pictures, ASTER GDEM (the only NASA 30 m map north of 60 N).
